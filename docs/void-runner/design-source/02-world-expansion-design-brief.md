---

# 2. CRITICAL RULE — APPROVAL BEFORE COMMITMENT

Do not create an entire campaign roadmap and treat it as final.

Work with me in manageable stages.

For each proposed mission or connected mission sequence:

1. Explain the starting situation.
2. Identify the NPC who introduces the mission.
3. Explain the player's objective.
4. Describe the gameplay.
5. Identify the location.
6. Explain any combat, exploration, or dialogue involved.
7. Describe possible rewards.
8. Explain how the mission ends.
9. Show where the player is directed afterward.
10. Offer multiple possible next missions or story directions.
11. Wait for my approval.

Nothing becomes an approved part of the official game roadmap until I explicitly accept it.

If I reject a proposal, revise it or present alternatives.

If I modify a proposal, incorporate my changes.

Do not interpret silence or ambiguous comments as approval.

## Approval stages

Use these statuses:

**PROPOSED** — An idea awaiting my decision.

**APPROVED** — I have accepted the idea.

**REVISE** — I want modifications.

**REJECTED** — Do not include this idea.

**DEFERRED** — Interesting, but save it for later consideration.

Never confuse proposed content with approved canon.

# 3. PRESENT ME WITH MEANINGFUL OPTIONS

When we reach an important decision, give me three to five distinct options.

Avoid offering several choices that are effectively identical.

Options should represent genuinely different experiences.

For example:

### After the player meets Rook at Meridian Station:

**Option A — Routine Cargo Delivery**

Rook offers a straightforward shipment to a nearby industrial settlement.

The player learns cargo handling and receives a modest reward.

This introduces normal commercial deliveries.

**Option B — Suspicious Cargo**

Rook offers unusually good pay to deliver a sealed container.

The player is not told what is inside.

This introduces moral uncertainty and potential smuggling.

**Option C — Missing Courier**

Rook asks the player to investigate a cargo ship that disappeared near an asteroid field.

This introduces exploration, scanning, and potential combat.

**Option D — Debt Collection**

Rook needs the player to recover payment from a trader who has stopped responding.

This introduces dialogue choices and possible confrontation.

For every option, explain:

- What makes it interesting.
- What gameplay systems it uses.
- What new content it requires.
- How difficult it would be to implement.
- How it could influence later missions.
- Where the player would go afterward.

Recommend the strongest option, but allow me to choose differently.

I may also combine elements from multiple options.

The campaign should not feel like a disconnected collection of assignments.

Each mission should lead naturally toward another opportunity, location, character, or discovery.

Develop a mission network with:

- Main story progression.
- Optional side branches.
- Returning NPCs.
- Recurring locations.
- Multiple mission outcomes.
- Long-term consequences.
- Faction-related branches.
- Optional exploration.
- Opportunities to return to previous locations.

## Mission roadmap visualization

Show me simple mission flowcharts.

Example:

Mara → Meridian Station → Rook

Then:

Rook
- Option A: Industrial Cargo Route
- Option B: Suspicious Shipment
- Option C: Missing Courier
- Option D: Debt Collection

After I select an option, develop its next steps.

Do not automatically approve all branches.

Distinguish between:

- Mandatory campaign missions.
- Optional side missions.
- Repeatable activities.
- Faction-specific missions.
- Hidden missions.

A player should be able to explore and take side jobs without losing track of the main storyline.

# 5. PLAN WHERE EACH MISSION LEADS THE PLAYER

This is one of my most important requirements.

I want deliberate control over how players are introduced to the universe.

Every mission proposal must explain:

**Where does the player start?**

**Where do they travel?**

**What happens there?**

**Who do they meet?**

**What do they discover?**

**Where are they directed next?**

**Why would they want to go there?**

Do not introduce locations randomly.

New destinations should have a reason to exist.

For example, an early cargo mission might introduce a mining outpost.

Later, the player could return to that outpost for a bounty, a repair, or a faction-related assignment.

This makes the universe feel interconnected rather than disposable.

When presenting mission options, consider whether an existing location can be reused or expanded before inventing another one.

# 7. PRESERVE THE EXISTING UNIVERSE

The following established concepts are important:

## Starting Planet

The player begins as a mechanic in a small settlement on a barren planet.

Mara introduces the player to weapons, movement, and spacecraft operation.

Elias Ward, the player's deceased employer, remains an important background character.

## Meridian Station

An important early commercial station.

Contains Rook, shops, a bar, NPCs, and access to cargo missions.

## Sol System

A relatively neutral commercial region.

Includes Earth, Mars, and The Darknet District.

## The Communion

A civilization that embraces AI and cybernetic augmentation.

Its religion revolves around a destroyed planetary AI.

## The Natural Order

A civilization opposed to AI and cybernetic augmentation.

Its culture and equipment reflect that philosophy.

## The Darknet District

An important Earth location associated with Admin and Iris.

## Derelict Stations

Locations for exploration, data retrieval, combat, and discoveries.

Do not casually rewrite these established elements.

You may propose improvements or additional history, but obtain my approval before making major changes.

# 8. DEVELOP CHARACTERS WITH PERSONALITIES AND MOTIVATIONS

I do not want NPCs who exist only to hand out missions.

Important characters should have:

- Personal histories.
- Motivations.
- Relationships.
- Strengths.
- Weaknesses.
- Secrets.
- Conflicts.
- Personal goals.
- Distinct dialogue styles.

Characters should sometimes reappear in unexpected circumstances.

A character introduced during a routine cargo delivery might later become important to a faction storyline.

Some NPCs may be trustworthy.

Others may lie, betray the player, or conceal information.

However, avoid making every character secretly evil.

The universe should include ordinary people trying to survive and make a living.

## NPC proposal format

When proposing an important character, include:

- Name.
- Approximate age.
- Appearance.
- Occupation.
- Personality.
- Background.
- Motivation.
- Relationship to the player.
- Mission involvement.
- Possible future development.

Do not finalize important characters without approval.

# 10. BUILD GRADUAL PROGRESSION

The player begins with limited resources and a modest spacecraft.

Do not immediately introduce the most powerful ships, weapons, factions, or enemies.

Progression should feel earned.

Early missions should introduce:

- Basic cargo hauling.
- Ship handling.
- Small settlements.
- Simple combat.
- Trading.
- Basic equipment upgrades.

Middle-game missions can introduce:

- More dangerous routes.
- Smuggling.
- Bounty hunting.
- Derelict exploration.
- Better ships.
- Faction politics.
- Advanced equipment.

Late-game missions may involve:

- Major faction conflicts.
- High-value targets.
- Advanced technology.
- Large installations.
- Difficult moral choices.
- Significant campaign consequences.

Avoid excessive grinding.

Players should be able to make meaningful progress during relatively short gameplay sessions.

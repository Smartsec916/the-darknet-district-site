# VOID//RUNNER — ADMIN'S INTRODUCTION TO WARFLYING

Location: The Darknet District, NeoChinatown, Tessaly System
NPC: Admin
Trigger: Player returns after completing the sealed cargo delivery to Rook ("Don't Ask Questions").
Tone: Admin is pragmatic, slightly cynical, dryly humorous, and knowledgeable about electronic privacy.

ADMIN:
"So, you made it back. Good. Rook said everything arrived."

ADMIN:
"Lucky you."

PLAYER:
"Lucky?"

ADMIN:
"You ever hear of WarFlying?"

PLAYER:
"Can't say I have."

ADMIN:
"It's a hobby. People fly around scanning electronic signals. Ships, stations, satellites, navigation beacons. Anything broadcasting something interesting."

ADMIN:
"Every new signal they discover earns them a point. There's even a leaderboard. People get surprisingly competitive about it."

PLAYER:
"Doesn't sound particularly dangerous."

ADMIN:
"That's because you're thinking about the people collecting points."

ADMIN:
"Now think about the people looking at the information."

ADMIN:
"Every time someone scans your ship, they might record your electronic signature, where you were, and when you were there."

ADMIN:
"One sighting doesn't tell much of a story. A hundred sightings? Suddenly someone knows where you travel, which stations you visit, and how often you make a particular run."

PLAYER:
"Who would care about that?"

ADMIN:
"Police. Bounty hunters. Pirates. Competitors. Anyone with enough curiosity and questionable intentions."

ADMIN:
"Some of those hobbyists upload their discoveries to The Relay. Wonderful little network. News, advertisements, leaderboards, and a convenient record of who's been seen where."

PLAYER:
"So anyone can track me?"

ADMIN:
"Not exactly. They're sightings, not a live tracking beacon. But give someone enough sightings and they can start making educated guesses."

ADMIN:
"Of course, there are ways to make your ship harder to identify."

PLAYER:
"Such as?"

ADMIN:
"WarFlying scramblers. They interfere with the electronic signatures those scanners use to identify ships."

ADMIN:
"Won't make you invisible. Won't stop a police officer from looking through your cockpit window. But it can make life considerably more frustrating for anyone trying to piece together your travel history."

PLAYER:
"You're selling these, aren't you?"

ADMIN:
"Naturally."

ADMIN:
"Scanner if you want to join the game. Scrambler if you'd rather not be part of someone else's."

ADMIN:
"One utility slot, though. You'll have to decide which matters more."

PLAYER:
"And you think I need a scrambler?"

ADMIN:
"After that last delivery? I'd say you got lucky."

## GAMEPLAY INTEGRATION

After this conversation:

* Introduce WarFlying and The Relay through the player's journal or information system.
* Make the WarFlying Scrambler available for purchase from Admin.
* Explain that the WarFlying Module and Scrambler use the same single ship utility slot.
* Do not automatically equip or purchase either module.
* Preserve the mysterious contents of Admin's original shipment.
* Do not reveal that the shipment was illegal or make the player wanted.
* Keep the initial delivery completely trouble-free.

Important: This dialogue introduces privacy concerns without forcing the player to become a pirate, criminal, bounty hunter, or WarFlying enthusiast. The player decides how to use the information.

# VOID//RUNNER — Babylon foundation report

This is an opt-in, playable migration foundation built on the working production game. The original renderer remains the default and recovery path. No production push, merge or deployment was performed.

Recovery checkpoint: `checkpoint/pre-babylon-production` at `0d7f43d`. Working branch: `codex/void-runner-babylon-foundation`.

Open `void-runner.html?renderer=babylon`, or choose **Settings → Graphics → Babylon 3D / preview**, then depart. Use `?renderer=legacy` for the original presentation. At a dock choose **Leave ship / 3D hangar**. Sol becomes available after the opening campaign reaches its existing open-job stage, with no active cargo contract.

## Requested report

1. **Changes:** Added a locally hosted, lazy-loaded Babylon renderer and state adapter; graphics settings; preparation/recovery screens; walking controller/interactions; small hangar, Sacramento and Mars blockouts; Sol expedition navigation; reusable escort state, billboards, local GLB pipeline and radio/music file playback hooks.
2. **Preserved systems:** Campaign progression, missions, credit economy, hull/equipment definitions, purchases, account integration, local/cloud save schema, story/dialogue/portraits, targeting, missiles, enemy AI, damage and the Kestrel flight integrator remain authoritative. Only narrow integration changes were made to existing modules.
3. **Migrated rendering:** In Babylon mode, actual meshes render planets, rocks, provisional station/ship forms, friendly/neutral traffic, lasers and missiles. Explosions combine additive textured billboards, expanding rings, smoke and pooled 3D fragments. Existing Canvas HUD, cockpit, departure painting and warp streaks remain composited overlays.
4. **Reused assets:** Canonical Kestrel cockpit; four painted sky environments; transparent departure/hangar art; character portrait/dialogue artwork. Existing fleet/station art and prompt documents guide provisional shapes and remain available to the original renderer. No canonical artwork was deleted.
5. **Placeholders:** All new ship/station meshes, walkable hangar, NPC bodies, Sacramento streets/alley/landing area and Mars subsurface access are blockouts. Earth/Mars orbital surfaces are procedural impressions. No finished TDD interior, hidden areas, merchandise designs or supplied music tracks were invented.
6. **Flight:** Preserved the existing 1/120-second internal integration, thrust, damping, yaw/pitch/roll rates and ship tiers. No Babylon physics plugin or second simulation loop. Babylon projection agrees with targeting math within 0.01 pixels in tests. Added modest bounded cockpit vibration while retaining motion-reduction behavior; a fully modeled cockpit, reflections and elaborate acceleration/recoil animation remain future polish.
7. **Graphics:** Real perspective and lighting, textured sphere planets, irregular instanced asteroids, lit station/ship geometry, projectiles, transparent emissive signs and bounded explosion effects. This is a functional 3D prototype, not finished industrial-science-fiction art. Dynamic shadows, screen-space reflections and expensive post-processing are deliberately absent.
8. **Audio:** Existing distance/pan-aware engines, weapons, missiles, explosions and voice behavior remain. Added walking footstep/door cues and local-file music/radio crossfades through the existing single AudioContext and volume buses. Procedural music yields to provided tracks. Radio has regional/story-conditioned availability; its station catalogs are empty until owner-provided audio is registered.
9. **Performance:** Engine download deferred until first 3D use; GLB loader deferred until a model is registered. One active environment, shared materials, instanced stars/rocks/projectiles/fragments, capped effects, and explicit scene/texture/model disposal. Three resolution/detail presets; conservative mobile/low-core default. Menu startup remained about 327 ms in the local unrelated-download-blocking test. This is not a physical-device GPU benchmark or a claim that every new scene is faster than the original.
10. **Departure:** Works in the tested Babylon path. The exterior scene is prepared and its assets/scene readiness checked before the departure clock begins. Planet, station and asteroid meshes exist behind the original transparent departure art from the first rendered frame. Failure holds departure with retry/recovery instead of showing an empty exterior.
11. **Waypoint delay:** Works. Two active seconds, then a 0.4-second fade. Resets at departure/area/encounter transitions and pauses in menus. Hidden-marker alignment cannot prematurely trigger warp. Destinations remain known internally.
12. **Missiles/combat:** Existing missile ownership, lock, range, launch, ammunition and swept impact work in Babylon tests. The input-driven pilot completed all four opening routes using real flight and laser/combat logic, reached `quest: open` and retained positive hull throughout. No health, damage or route-progress cheats were used in that piloted suite.
13. **Escorts:** Physical friendly shuttle state integrates with existing escort contracts. It navigates along the route heading, waits when the pilot is too far away, reports distress, takes enemy projectile damage, fails safely on destruction, and requires proximity/progress/hostile clearance before completion. Browser tests cover damage, progress, waiting and the existing completion hook. Convoys and independent long-distance pathfinding are not implemented.
14. **Walking:** Works with WASD, Shift, mouse capture/drag look, flat-floor collision, boundary limits and touch controls. It has no visible player body, jumping, stairs or crouch. Collision uses small movement substeps to avoid tunneling through the blockout walls.
15. **Rook:** The physical hangar interaction opens his existing quest-sensitive job/dialogue flow. The player can return to walking and board the Kestrel. NPC meshes are placeholders; the existing dialogue portrait remains canonical.
16. **Sol:** Reachable after the opening campaign with an interstellar alignment/transit sequence. It exposes Earth and Mars, stores the last visited destination in existing validated story flags, and offers return to the frontier. This is a compact expedition layer; it does not pretend Sol is another nearby station or replace canonical station IDs.
17. **Earth/Sacramento:** Small collidable landing/street/alley blockout with city signage and a TDD entrance interaction. The entrance explicitly stops before the future security vestibule/interior. No whole Sacramento reconstruction.
18. **Mars:** Small landing/access/airlock/subsurface terminal blockout with a distinct palette and underground-city signposting. Future market, residential, industrial and transit spaces remain content work.
19. **Tests:** 46 JavaScript unit tests, 23 Python tests, fifteen pre-existing browser suites and four new Babylon browser suites passed. JavaScript syntax checks and whitespace checks (respecting the production CRLF files) passed. Both static and Flask entry points rendered Babylon successfully. See the detailed test list below.
20. **Remaining limitations:** Prototype geometry/art fidelity; no fully modeled cockpit/TDD; flat-floor walking; explicit short preparation screens during warp/location changes; no exact walking-position or mid-interstellar-transit save; escort restarts from the existing route checkpoint on reload; local radio picks the first eligible program rather than scheduling playlists; no physical-phone performance certification or live Google/payment transaction test. The 2D/GPU composition copy remains a possible future performance optimization.
21. **Recommended next step:** Review this branch as a preview. Author one canonical Kestrel GLB and one Meridian exterior/hangar kit from the existing artwork, replace their registered blockouts, and profile the same departure/combat/walking tests on a physical low-end phone and desktop. Design the TDD vestibule/interior separately before implementing it.
